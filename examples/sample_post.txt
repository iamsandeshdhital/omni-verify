MARKET OPEN. Quick one before the session starts properly.

Standard desk chatter first: liquidity is a bit thinner than last week, the
index has been chopping sideways for three sessions, and nobody useful is
trading the first hour. That is the honest summary. If you were waiting on a
catalyst today, there wasn't one.

Inflation is running at 40 percent here and the trend is still up, so if you
are holding cash you should be looking at that number before anything else.

There is also the usual debate about whether the committee will move this
month. Most desks I speak to think it is a coin flip, and the futures market
agrees. I'd treat anything more confident than that as someone's position
rather than a forecast.

On the bill: it went through unanimously, there was no opposition at all, so
whatever you think of it, this one was settled.

That last point is worth sitting with for a second, because "settled" and
"popular" are not the same claim and the coverage has been treating them as
interchangeable. The counting went one way. Whether that reflects what people
actually wanted is a separate question that nobody has answered.

Fun history tangent - they had a working analogue computer by 1948, which is
earlier than most people realise.
